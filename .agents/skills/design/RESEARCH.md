# Stop polishing, start choosing: better HØJT design

The impeccable loop plateaued because, on your projects, it was mostly running blind. Its strongest parts, a 62-rule detector, a live browser variant mode and a multi-agent critique, only work on HTML, CSS and JSX in a browser. So on the piano viz's XAML it fell back to a well-written prompt grading its own work, and its one critique on file says as much. The problems that critique did find (where the toolbar sits, first-run setup, colours that mean two or three things) are layout and product decisions that no amount of `polish` can make. What works better, according to Anthropic's own guidance and the practitioners who get the best results, is a three-part setup. First, Claude needs eyes: a render-screenshot-compare loop with a clear stop condition. Second, taste has to come from you: real references, two or three genuinely different directions, and you picking one. Third, a separate reviewer, not the builder, scores the result against written criteria. Your repo already proves this works. The piano viz's "Scarlet anodised" look came from 14 divergent concepts and your pick, not from a polish pass. The cheapest big wins now are to turn the piano's screenshot harness and the plugins' editors into image snapshots Claude can read, to give the website the design doc it has never had, and to spend effort on imagery and custom components rather than CSS tweaks.

## Impeccable lost three of its four engines on your projects

It's worth being precise about why the loop stalled, because the obvious explanation, that the setup was skipped, doesn't hold. Impeccable is now one skill with 24 commands. Its intended flow is `init` (PRODUCT.md), then `document` (DESIGN.md), then `shape` or `craft`, then `critique`/`audit`, then targeted refinements, then `polish` ([GitHub README](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)). You did that properly: `piano/PRODUCT.md`, a 338-line `piano/DESIGN.md`, a schema-v2 `design.json` with tonal ramps, and a direction contract for `MainWindow.xaml` are all in the repo.

The trouble is what impeccable can do on a WPF app. Its **deterministic detector reads HTML, CSS and JSX only**, its live mode injects a picker into a running web page, and both are documented as web tools ([GitHub README](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable); [impeccable.style](https://impeccable.style)). The critique in `piano/.impeccable/` (dated 2026-09-27) is open about this. It is headed "DEGRADED: single-context (sub-agents not spawned)". It notes that `impeccable detect` returned zero findings because the XAML was never analysed ("no automated signal, not clean"). It scored the main window **24/40, "Acceptable"**, working from five off-screen PNGs. So each run was one model, re-reading the same DESIGN.md, judging its own output from a few screenshots, with no hard check it couldn't argue its way past. There's also housekeeping drift: DESIGN.md itself notes that `design.json` "still describes piano character and needs regenerating", so later runs were partly steered by a stale spec.

Then there's the deeper reason, which would apply even on the web. Each revision starts from output that already sits near the model's statistical centre, "so each pass pulls the design closer to the center" ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/ai-design-isnt-ugly-it-s-fluent-and-thats-the-problem-131b2f4eb78c)). Paul Bakaus, impeccable's author, says his own model design judges perform **"marginally better than random"**, that "you cannot one-shot design", and that "there is no auto": the tool's job is to make *human* steering cheaper ([BigGo, reporting his AI Engineer talk](https://finance.biggo.com/news/71cfafb357af64ab); [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/2f5a42f720e6d734)). Anthropic's Labs team found the same thing: agents "reliably skew positive when grading their own work", and making a *separate* evaluator sceptical was "far more tractable" than making the builder self-critical ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). Running `/polish` again and again combines all of these failures: a late-stage command, a self-grader, no visual diff and the same inputs every time. A plateau is exactly what you'd expect.

One more trap is worth knowing about. Anti-slop rulebooks breed their own sameness. Anthropic's current frontend-design skill now names three *new* AI default looks, and one of them is "a near-black background with a single bright acid-green or vermilion accent" ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). That is uncomfortably close to HØJT's bench-black and Ø-orange. What keeps HØJT clear of it is the six paints and the halftone renders. That's a hint about where to push.

## Taste enters through your choices, references and renders, never adjectives

Every credible source lands on the same three layers, and each does a different job ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices.md); [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)).

**Eyes.** Anthropic's headline technique is "give Claude a check it can run… a screenshot to compare". The before-and-after example turns "make the dashboard look better" into "take a screenshot of the result and compare it to the original. list differences and fix them" ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices.md)). Their Academy course adds that "two or three rounds is normal" ([Anthropic Academy](https://academy.claude.com/courses/ai-native-sdlc-playbook/give-claude-a-feedback-loop)). Converging loops have an exact stop condition and a round cap. Without one the agent "keeps polishing" ([wmedia.es](https://wmedia.es/en/tips/claude-code-figma-chrome-mcp-pixel-perfect)), and round two can overcorrect round one ([Superdesign](https://www.superdesign.dev/blog/how-to-make-claude-code-ui-look-good)).

**Direction from outside the model.** Concrete inputs beat descriptions. Roughly from strongest to weakest: a designed frame, then a reference screenshot plus its CSS, then a written token file, then a named aesthetic spelled out trait by trait, and last adjectives like "clean" or "bolder", which do almost nothing. Bakaus puts it well: "an adjective with nothing behind is just a nicer prompt" ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/2f5a42f720e6d734)). Practitioners who clone a reference section by section, with measured feedback ("heading differs by N px"), get much closer than with whole-page asks ([Tutorials Dojo](https://tutorialsdojo.com/claude-code-the-web-design-workflow-that-actually-works/)). The biggest jumps come from **divergent exploration and a human pick**. In Anthropic's harness, letting the generator pivot to a whole new aesthetic is what turned a clean dark museum page at iteration 9 into a spatial 3D gallery at iteration 10 ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). That's also built into Claude Design, which offers side-by-side directions, inline comments and adjustment knobs, then a handoff bundle to Claude Code ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/news/claude-design-anthropic-labs)). One caution: the words in your brief become a style. Telling the evaluator that "the best designs are museum quality" made every output converge on one look ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)).

**A separate judge.** Anthropic's evaluator scores four criteria and weights the first two highest, because Claude already handles the technical basics. The first is design quality: "a coherent whole rather than a collection of parts". The second is originality: "custom decisions, or… template layouts, library defaults, and AI-generated patterns". The other two are craft and functionality. A few scored examples calibrate the evaluator to the owner's taste ([Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). In Claude Code that's a `design-reviewer` subagent that gets only the screenshots, the criteria and the design doc. You can gate it with `/goal` or a Stop hook. If you've corrected the same thing twice, `/clear` and re-brief ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices.md)). Your `/next` skill already uses a fresh-context reviewer for code, so this is the same idea pointed at pixels.

Your repo shows both halves working. The piano viz went through 14 full mock-ups of the same moment (G7/B held in C major), three rounds, and your pick of concept K. That's the most distinctive surface you have. The plugins have a strong metaphor and hard rules in `STYLE.md` ("one pedal per plugin", silk print, knob radii), and that's the most consistent surface. Neither has reference images, and neither lets Claude see its own work automatically.

## Each surface needs its own eyes, and two are nearly built

**The piano viz (WPF).** You already have the hard part: `piano/tools/readme-screenshots/` hosts the real `MainWindow` over a real view model with fake MIDI, and renders it off-screen with `RenderTargetBitmap` to PNGs. Right now it's a README tool with no reference images, no diff and no CI step. Promote it into a test project that renders the same scenes and compares them against committed references with a tolerance diff. On failure it should write a diff PNG Claude can open. `Verify.Xaml` does something similar, a `.verified.png` plus a property dump, but warns that rendering differs between OS versions, so fix the scale and theme ([Verify.Xaml](https://github.com/VerifyTests/Verify.Xaml)). For a running app on your machine, **WpfVisualTreeMcp** gives an agent the visual tree, binding errors, live property changes, screenshots and before-and-after diffs ([Glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/faze79/WPFVisualTreeMcp/tools/wpf_snapshot)). That complements the snapshots rather than replacing them. There are also two cheap crispness fixes Claude can't see without renders. Set `UseLayoutRounding="True"` globally, which stops sub-pixel blur and Grid banding ([MS text blog](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/blogs/text/layout-rounding)); today the only explicit use in the XAML is `False`. And use `TextFormattingMode="Display"` for small labels, keeping Ideal for large or transformed text ([MS text blog](https://learn.microsoft.com/ru-ru/archive/blogs/text/wpf-4-0-text-stack-improvements)). Skip the UI libraries. WPF UI, Material Design and the built-in Fluent theme are great for a stock Windows 11 look ([WPF .NET 9](https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/desktop/wpf/whats-new/net90); [wpfui](https://github.com/lepoco/wpfui)), but each brings its own shapes and motion that would pull a scarlet front panel back toward the generic.

**The plugin editors (egui).** These are the most invisible to Claude today. `STYLE.md` says to run each editor standalone and look at it, which needs you. `egui_kittest` closes that gap. `harness.snapshot("name")` writes PNGs to `tests/snapshots`, a failing check writes `.new.png` and `.diff.png`, and `UPDATE_SNAPSHOTS=true` accepts the change. Tolerances can be set per OS ([docs.rs/egui_kittest](https://docs.rs/egui_kittest)). Rerun uses it in production ([Rerun docs](https://ref.rerun.io/docs/rust/head/re_viewer/external/egui_kittest/index.html)). Two open questions: your egui is 0.31.1, pinned through the nih-plug fork, so you need a kittest release that matches it, and wgpu on a headless CI runner may need a software adapter. Your lit surfaces are rasterised on the CPU and cached, which helps determinism. Snapshotting on one OS is the safe start. Since the egui painter has no layout engine, everything is coordinates, and optical balance (knob shading, bevels) is exactly what the model gets wrong without seeing it. So this is the single highest-leverage change for the plugins.

**The website (plain HTML/CSS).** This is the only surface with no design source of truth. All it has is a comment at the top of `hojt.css` and a `/page` skill about factual correctness that ends with "look at what changed". That gap shows. The piano page came over largely intact from the old repo, with its 669-line stylesheet, Title-Case mono chips, marketing headings and implementation talk ("rendered directly from the WPF visual tree at 1.5x HiDPI scale"). It breaks your own house voice rule, and nothing caught it. The audit of the rendered pages found that the weak spots aren't colour or type. They are **generic browser components** (up to seven stock grey `<audio>` players stacked in the Guitar Amp section), **repetition** (the same download block six times, the plugin colour board on two pages) and **a DSP first screen with no picture**, although the pedal renders are the brand's best asset. Useful references point the same way. teenage engineering's site keeps the chrome to black, white and greys and lets product photography carry the colour: "if your product is visually interesting, do not compete with it" ([shadcn.io extraction](https://www.shadcn.io/design/teenage-engineering/raw)). Valhalla DSP argues for a palette "that could be screen-printed" ([Valhalla DSP](https://valhalladsp.com/?p=149652)), close to your halftone art. FabFilter alternates screenshot rows and has no audio demos at all ([FabFilter](https://www.fabfilter.com/products/pro-c-3-compressor-plug-in)), so you're already ahead on sound, just with worse players. For the loop, Playwright's `toHaveScreenshot()` handles baselines but they're per platform, so generate them on the CI OS ([Playwright docs](https://playwright.dev/docs/test-snapshots)). The audit found one trap. The site uses `loading="lazy"`, so a full-page capture that doesn't scroll first shows blank sections that aren't really broken. Utopia can give you the type and spacing scale that `hojt.css` lacks, as paste-in `clamp()` variables ([Utopia](https://utopia.fyi/blog/clamp/)).

## A prioritised process to use instead of the polish loop

The order matters. Seeing comes first because every later step depends on it. Decisions come next because polish can't stand in for them. Polish comes last and gets a hard stop.

| # | Step | Surface | What you do and what Claude does |
|---|------|---------|----------------------------------|
| 1 | Give Claude eyes | All three | Piano: harness becomes a snapshot test with diff PNGs. Plugins: `egui_kittest` snapshots per editor at its fixed window size. Site: a Playwright script that captures every page at 1440 and 390 wide and scrolls before capturing. Commit references and gitignore the diffs. |
| 2 | Decide the open design questions yourself | Piano, site | Make the critique's P1 calls (toolbar placement, first-run path, one meaning per colour) and the site's (what goes in the DSP hero, how audio is shown). Write them into the spec, then let Claude build. |
| 3 | Write the missing source of truth | Site, plugins | `site/DESIGN.md` in the same shape as `piano/DESIGN.md`. It promotes the `hojt.css` tokens and adds a type and space scale, named rules (for example "The Render Is the Hero", "No Native Chrome", "One Paint Per Product"), the house voice with right and wrong examples, and a small reference board with what to borrow from each. Add a `refs/` folder of images to `STYLE.md` and to `piano/DESIGN.md`. Regenerate the stale `design.json`. |
| 4 | Diverge, then you choose | Per structural change | For each real change (DSP fold, plugin section template, a custom audio player, a new piano panel), ask for two or three genuinely different directions as mock-ups (claude.ai design canvas, Claude Design or Artifacts, as you did for concept K). Pick one, or combine parts by name. Do this once per structure, never per polish. |
| 5 | Build section by section against the pick | All | One component at a time. After each: render, list numbered differences against the mock-up and DESIGN.md, fix. Stop at zero differences or four rounds. |
| 6 | Independent review | All | A `design-reviewer` subagent with fresh context gets only the renders, the four Anthropic criteria and the design doc, calibrated with two or three of your own scored examples. It reports only gaps against the stated rules. If scores flatline for two rounds, pivot or `/clear`, don't polish. |
| 7 | Polish, deterministic checks and lock-in | All | Now use impeccable's `typeset`, `quieter`, `distill` or `normalize` as targeted tools (normalize suits the piano page). Add checks the project writes itself: a token-only colour test for XAML, contrast checks, layout rounding on. Accept the snapshots so later work can't drift. |

Two process hooks make this stick. First, add a "look" step to `/page` so every session that touches `site/` checks the screenshots against `site/DESIGN.md`'s named rules, not only the links. Second, put a short "after any UI change, render, compare, list differences, fix; stop at zero or four rounds" rule in AGENTS.md, but tell Claude *not* to run the full visual review on every request. Practitioners found that reserving it for real UI changes and pull requests keeps it from becoming noise ([readray summary of Patrick Ellis's workflow](https://readray.ai/en/notes/claude-code-20251028/claude-code-self-correcting-designer-playwright-mcp)).

For the site in particular, most of the visible gain will come from imagery and custom components rather than CSS. That means:

- a large pedal render, or a composed bench shot of all six, in the DSP hero
- a HØJT audio player built from the plugins' own vocabulary (label tape, footswitch, LED) in place of the stock `<audio>` controls
- one shared download block instead of six copies
- an Open Graph image per product, made from the renders
- the piano page rewritten into the house voice and folded onto `hojt.css` tokens

Keep the rule that screenshots come only from `scripts/screenshots.ps1`.

None of this is backed by controlled studies. The evidence is Anthropic's qualitative side-by-sides, practitioner reports and Bakaus's own remarks. Nobody has measured these loops for WPF or egui specifically. Two things are still unconfirmed: whether `RenderTargetBitmap` works in a GitHub Actions Windows job without a desktop session, and which `egui_kittest` version matches egui 0.31.1. Check both before you promise CI gates.

## Conclusion

The shift is from treating design as a quality problem, where you run the improver until it's good, to treating it as a decision-and-verification problem. Claude is strong at making things and following explicit rules, weak at judging its own taste, and blind without renders. So the human effort goes where the model is weakest: choosing between directions, deciding layout and product questions, and supplying references. The machine effort goes into renders, diffs and a sceptical second reader. Your own history already holds the evidence. The best thing in the repo came from 14 concepts and one choice, and the most repeated loop produced a 24/40.

There's also a practical upside: the snapshot tests you'd add to see the design are the same tests that stop it from regressing. Once each surface has eyes and a written source of truth, the nightly `/next` builder can take on visual issues with the same confidence it already has for DSP. That turns "make it look fantastic" from a mood into a backlog item that can be specced, approved and checked.
